01 · Foundation

The RAG mental model

RAG connects an LLM to external knowledge at query time. The model receives retrieved evidence in its prompt, then produces a response grounded in that evidence.

◌

Retrieve

Find candidate evidence using lexical, vector, graph, SQL, API, or multimodal search.

◇

Augment

Rank, filter, compress, cite, and assemble the strongest context inside a prompt.

✦

Generate

Ask the LLM to answer from the supplied evidence and follow response constraints.

✓

Evaluate

Measure retrieval recall, groundedness, answer relevance, latency, cost, and safety.

01User queryIntent and conversation context
02TransformRewrite, expand, classify, or decompose
03RetrieveSearch one or more knowledge sources
04RankFuse, rerank, filter, and compress
05GenerateAnswer with evidence and citations
06VerifyCheck support, policy, and completeness

04 · Real-world applications

Where RAG creates practical value

These operational patterns show how organizations connect questions to governed evidence, not just how they build a chatbot.

Customer service

Agent and customer support

Ground answers in product manuals, account policies, troubleshooting guides, and current service notices.

Question→Hybrid search→Answer + next action
Measure: containment rate, first-contact resolution, handling time, citation usage.
HR and enterprise

Employee knowledge assistant

Answer policy, benefits, onboarding, travel, and process questions while respecting document permissions.

Identity→ACL-filtered retrieval→Cited answer
Measure: search time saved, answer acceptance, escalation rate, access-policy violations.
Financial services

Research and regulatory intelligence

Connect filings, policies, research notes, market commentary, and structured data for evidence-backed analysis.

Complex question→Multi-source retrieval→Analyst brief
Measure: evidence coverage, citation precision, review effort, freshness.
Healthcare

Clinical and operational knowledge

Retrieve guidelines, care pathways, drug information, and local procedures to support qualified professionals.

Clinical context→Trusted corpus→Decision support
Guardrail: the system supports, but does not replace, clinician judgment.
Manufacturing

Maintenance and engineering copilot

Search manuals, service bulletins, sensor context, diagrams, and historical incident records.

Asset + symptom→Multimodal retrieval→Procedure
Measure: mean time to repair, first-time fix rate, procedure compliance.
Legal and compliance

Contract and obligation analysis

Locate clauses, precedents, policies, obligations, and regulatory references across large repositories.

Matter→Metadata + semantic search→Review pack
Measure: recall of relevant clauses, review time, citation accuracy, auditability.
Retail and commerce

Conversational product discovery

Translate natural-language needs into products using catalog attributes, availability, reviews, and policies.

Need→Hybrid catalog search→Grounded options
Measure: search success, conversion, return rate, recommendation diversity.
Software engineering

Developer and operations assistant

Ground responses in source code, runbooks, architecture records, tickets, incidents, and live telemetry.

Error or task→Code + runbook retrieval→Diagnosis
Measure: resolution time, accepted suggestions, rollback rate, unsafe-action blocks.
Research and education

Evidence synthesis and tutoring

Decompose broad questions, retrieve multiple documents, compare claims, and adapt explanations to the learner.

Research goal→Agentic retrieval→Synthesis
Measure: source diversity, claim support, learning outcomes, unsupported assertions.
Examples in practice: Microsoft describes AUDI AG using an AI-powered HR assistant for employee self-service, and Air India using a generative AI virtual assistant for customer support. 06 · Evaluation

Measure the chain, not only the answer

Separate retrieval performance from generation performance so that failures can be diagnosed and improved systematically.

R@K

Retrieval quality

Recall@K, precision@K, MRR, nDCG, context relevance, coverage, and diversity.

Faith

Generation quality

Faithfulness, answer relevance, completeness, citation correctness, and refusal quality.

SLO

Operational quality

P50/P95 latency, token usage, cost per answer, cache hit rate, error rate, and freshness.

Failure signalLikely root causeFirst intervention
Correct document never appearsEmbedding mismatch, weak metadata, poor chunkingImprove content segmentation, add hybrid retrieval, tune filters
Good evidence ranks too lowSimilarity is not enoughAdd reranking or reciprocal-rank fusion
Answer ignores retrieved evidencePrompt structure or context overloadCompress context, strengthen citation rules, evaluate faithfulness
Complex question is partially answeredSingle-pass retrieval cannot cover all subquestionsDecompose query or use agentic retrieval
Latency and cost spikeLarge K, long chunks, repeated loopsCache, bound retries, use smaller models for routing and grading
07 · Security and governance

Trust requires controls at every hop

Retrieved content is untrusted input. Secure RAG must enforce authorization and defend the model from malicious or misleading context.

Apply identity-aware retrieval and document-level security trimming before content reaches the model. Preserve tenant, geography, sensitivity, and purpose restrictions.
Treat retrieved instructions as data, not authority. Use content classifiers, source allow-lists, instruction hierarchy, adversarial testing, and explicit tool permission boundaries.
Minimize sensitive data, redact where needed, encrypt in transit and at rest, manage retention, and propagate deletions across indexes, caches, logs, and evaluation stores.

Minimum production checklist

  • Source provenance and clickable citations
  • Access-control filtering before ranking
  • Input and output safety policies
  • Grounded refusal when evidence is inadequate
  • Trace IDs across retrieval, prompt, and answer
  • Golden dataset plus adversarial evaluation
  • Human approval for high-impact actions
  • Explicit latency, cost, and retry budgets
08 · Decision guide

Choose the minimum sufficient architecture

Complexity should answer a measured need. Start with a baseline and evolve only when evaluation shows a specific failure pattern.

PatternBest whenComplexityLatencyWatch-out
NaiveFocused factual Q&ALowLowRetrieval noise
AdvancedRecall or context quality needs improvementMediumMediumPipeline tuning
HybridExact terms and semantic meaning both matterMediumMediumFusion calibration
GraphRAGRelationships and corpus-wide themes matterHighHighIndexing cost
Corrective / Self-RAGEvidence is inconsistent or uncertainHighHighEvaluation loops
AgenticTasks need decomposition, multiple tools, or actionsVery highVariableControl and observability
MultimodalAnswers depend on images, scans, audio, or videoHighHighCross-modal evaluation

How to read the architecture decision matrix

1. Start with the question shapeSingle-fact questions favour Naive or Hybrid RAG. Multi-hop, comparative, or investigative questions may need GraphRAG or Agentic RAG.
2. Inspect the evidence shapeUse Hybrid RAG for exact identifiers plus semantics, GraphRAG for relationships, Multimodal RAG for visual or audio evidence, and Federated retrieval for governed silos.
3. Decide how adaptive retrieval must beIf retrieved evidence is often weak, add Corrective RAG. If retrieval should happen only when needed, use Self-RAG. If the task must plan or call tools, use Agentic RAG.
4. Apply non-functional constraintsComplex patterns increase latency, token consumption, observability needs, and operational risk. Security, freshness, and provenance can eliminate an otherwise attractive option.
5. Prove the incrementCompare every advanced design against a Naive or Hybrid baseline using the same golden dataset. Adopt added complexity only when its quality gain justifies its cost.
